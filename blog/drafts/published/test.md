# Introductions.

![Jack Guo - Unsplash](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1629138144227-c157c21e1674?q=80&w=1734&auto=format&fit=crop&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&ixid=M3wxMjA3fDB8MHxwaG90by1wYWdlfHx8fGVufDB8fHx8fA%3D%3D)

My name is Darian. I'm an IT Systems Engineer. In the last decade I've spent a great deal of time architecting endpoint environments, building identity infrastructure (as code, sometimes) and automating everything I've had to touch more than once. I've come to realize that today devices, identity, security and automation all fully intersect.

I'm starting this blog to document all the problems I've resolved and hope to give insight to other readers. Over the past decade many blogs such as this one have been my saving grace in getting me across the finish line on some complex projects.

## What this blog is

This is where I write about the things I actually do. Not theory. Not product marketing. Not "10 Best Practices for Zero Trust" written by someone who's never configured a Conditional Access policy.  

These are detail-oriented posts about real IT projects, written to help other professionals. My hope is that coming here you will find posts on how to stand up useful features, or overcoming real problems that I've hit in production and how I solved them. GAM one-liners that save you from clicking through the Google Admin Console like it's 2015.
  
I want to write for the tech who's 4 nested links deep within a vendor's knowledge article, another tab with a Reddit thread from 2021 that almost details the full resolution to the issue they are facing.
  
I have opinions. Some tools are good. Some tools generate more tickets than they solve and I'll say that out loud. If you disagree, cool. I'd rather be honest than diplomatic.

## What this blog isn't
This isn't an MSP marketing blog. I'm not trying to sell you a retainer or scare you into thinking your environment is on fire so you'll call me. Maybe it is on fire. That's between you and your auditor lol.   
  
This isn't a place where I act like everything deploys clean on the first push. I break stuff. Ask me about the time I pushed a local admin policy in Intune before I confirmed my AD group was actually in the config. Locked myself right out.

## TL;DR
I plan to write about endpoint management, identity and access, security compliance, automation, and the weird gaps between all of them. Practical stuff, and the occasional IRL post. These are the kind of posts I wish existed when I was the one searching.
